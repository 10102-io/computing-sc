## 0. Ground truth that changes the two threads

Corrections to thread #1 (destination wallet):

1. **A router-only `payTo` cannot work.** `TimelockERC20.withdraw` pays
   `lock.recipient` from storage; the NFT vaults pay `caller`. The router
   passes `auth_.recipient` as `caller`. Redirecting funds needs a new
   vault entrypoint. (`TimeLockERC20.sol:194-226`, `TimeLockERC721.sol:178-201`,
   `TimeLockERC1155.sol:203-230`.)
2. **Vault `withdraw` is `external` and not `onlyRouter`.** Anyone may
   trigger a payout to the rightful recipient today, which is safe only
   because the payee is fixed. The destination override must therefore
   live in a NEW, `onlyRouter` entrypoint (`withdrawTo`), and the existing
   `withdraw` must stay byte-for-byte (payee fixed, not `onlyRouter`).
3. **The event already has four fields**:
   `TimelockWithdrawnFor(uint256 indexed id, address indexed recipient,
   address indexed relayer, uint256 timestamp)`. The subgraph does not
   index it at all; it indexes the vaults' `FundsWithdrawn` /
   `TokensWithdrawn` (`computing-subgraph/subgraph.yaml`). Do not change
   those. Add a new router event `TimelockWithdrawnTo` and keep the old
   one emitting.
4. **Four proxies, not one.** Router plus all three vaults change, all
   behind the 48h queue. `scripts/dump-storage-layouts.ts` currently omits
   the three vaults (`PROXIED_CONTRACTS`, lines 16-28); extend it first.
5. **There is no `AGENTS.md` and no `[Unreleased]` in this repo** (the
   thread told the agent to read both). `AGENTS.md` now exists (this
   commit); CHANGELOG stays dated-entries-only.
6. **No fuzz harness exists.** Tests are Hardhat + mocha. "Fuzz the
   recipient/payTo pair" means property tests with `fast-check` inside
   mocha, or a Foundry harness added deliberately; pick the former for
   this round.
7. **Sponsored test gaps to close anyway**: no NFT `withdrawFor` test, no
   swap path under `withdrawFor`, no soft lock under `withdrawFor`.

Corrections to thread #2 (governance):

1. **Live state confirms the premise**: all three vaults and the
   TimeLockRouter are owned by the maintainer EOA on mainnet and Sepolia;
   `DefaultProxyAdmin` is already owned by the UpgradeTimelock; PROPOSER
   and CANCELLER are the EOA, EXECUTOR is open, DEFAULT_ADMIN is the
   timelock itself; `getMinDelay()` is 172800 (mainnet) and 300 (Sepolia);
   `TimelockERC20.uniswapRouter()` is the canonical V2 router on both.
   No router fix is needed before step 1.
2. **`timelock-op.ts` has no batch.** Every grant, revoke and transfer
   would be its own operation and window. Add `scheduleBatch` /
   `executeBatch` to the script before running step 2, or the sequence
   multiplies coordination risk.
3. **Routers stay on a single EOA in the thread as written**, which leaves
   `TimeLockRouter.setUniswapRouter`, `setTimelock`, `setTokenWhitelist`
   and `setConsentVerifier` (all instant, `onlyOwner`) on one key. That is
   the same risk class the vault step removes. Recommendation: move the
   routers' Ownable ownership (and the EOA router's `codeAdmin`) to the
   **Safe**, not the timelock: multi-party and still minutes-fast, so
   `setCreatePaused` keeps working as a circuit breaker.
4. **One guardian is one point of failure.** If the guardian key is lost,
   nothing can cancel a malicious queue until the Safe schedules a new
   grant. Founder decision: start with one hardware guardian whose
   recovery phrase is stored elsewhere, and add a second by scheduled
   `grantRole` later.
5. **`updateDelay` is itself delayed at the current delay** and stretches
   everything scheduled after it, not operations already queued. The
   destination-wallet upgrade must be scheduled at 48h and executed
   before the 7-day change is scheduled.
6. **Proving "no pending operations" on free RPCs failed** in the audit
   (`eth_getLogs` unreliable). The reminder-worker now keeps a
   `timelock_ops` ledger and serves `GET /upgrades`; the verify script
   should read that, with a direct `isOperationPending(id)` check for any
   id it knows.
7. **Sepolia stays at 300s.** Document the asymmetry rather than mirror it.
8. **The public policy page and CHANGELOG say 48h.** The gift page reads
   the delay live, the rest does not; update `computing-docs/architecture/
   upgrade-policy.md`, the reminder-worker alert copy, and this repo's
   CHANGELOG on the day `updateDelay` executes.

## 3. Track B: the timelock train (4 proxies, one 48h window)

### B1. Destination wallet on sponsored gift claims (`gift-claim-destination`)

Why: before a claim, the recipient address is only a hash on chain; the
claim signature reveals its public key; today the funds then rest in that
address until a second, self-paid move. For 5- to 18-year locks that
resting period is the window a future quantum attacker would use. With a
destination the key signs once and the funds never rest there. It also
gives email-claimed gifts (Turnkey) a clean exit to the recipient's own
wallet, which closes most of `email-wallet-export`.

Design (replaces the thread's "add a field to the router" with what the
code allows):

- Router: `struct WithdrawAuth { address recipient; address payTo;
  uint256 nonce; uint256 deadline; bytes signature; }`, new typehash
  `WithdrawAuth(address recipient,address payTo,uint256 timelockId,bool
  skipSwap,uint256 nonce,uint256 deadline)`, domain version `"2"`.
  `payTo == address(0)` means pay the recorded recipient. Reject `payTo`
  equal to the router or any of the three vaults. No allow-list beyond
  that; the recipient's signature is the authority.
- Vaults: add `withdrawTo(uint256 id, address caller, address payTo,
  bool skipSwap)` on ERC20 and `withdrawTo(uint256 id, address caller,
  address payTo)` on ERC721/ERC1155, all `onlyRouter`. Authorisation
  stays `caller == lock.recipient`; only the transfer target changes:
  `safeTransfer`, the WETH unwrap and the Uniswap swap path pay `payTo`;
  NFT transfers pay `payTo`. The existing `withdraw` functions do not
  change by one byte.
- Router `withdrawFor` calls `withdrawTo(..., payTo == 0 ? recipient :
  payTo, ...)` on all three vaults and emits both the old event and
  `TimelockWithdrawnTo(uint256 indexed id, address indexed recipient,
  address indexed payTo, address relayer, uint256 timestamp)`.
- Direct, self-paid `withdraw(id)` and `withdraw(id, skipSwap)` untouched
  (decision recorded: sponsored-only; a wallet that pays its own gas can
  forward funds itself, and the direct path stays out of the review).
- Storage: no new variables anywhere. Typehash and version hash are
  constants. Document "no layout change" in the storage comment blocks.
- Tests (extend `test/SponsoredTimelockWithdraw.spec.ts`): payTo pays
  payTo for USDC, for a WETH-stored ETH gift with unwrap, for the swap
  path, for ERC721 and ERC1155, for a soft lock; `payTo == 0` identical to
  today; signature over the old typehash or old domain rejected; nonce
  consumed once, replay with a different payTo fails; payTo == router or
  a vault reverts; direct `withdraw` from a non-router caller with the
  new `withdrawTo` reverts (`onlyRouter`); existing direct-path tests keep
  passing. Property test with `fast-check`: for random (recipient, payTo,
  signer) triples, funds move only when the recovered signer equals
  `lock.recipient`.
- Deliverables: contract diff, tests green, storage-layout diff for all
  four contracts (extend `dump-storage-layouts.ts`), ABI artifacts,
  CHANGELOG entry, Sepolia deploy and rehearsal, and the consumer note in
  B3.

## 5. Track D: new contracts (no upgrade queue, each its own review)

- **D1. SavingsLadder** (the DCA idea, `computing/docs/plans/front-doors.md`
  Save door and the founder's Coinbase question): a per-user clone with
  owner, buckets `{durationMonths, shareBps}[]`, a minimum split amount,
  an empty `receive()`, and a permissionless `split()` that creates one
  `createTimelockedGift` per bucket with `recipient = owner` from the
  contract's balance (USDC as is; ETH through the router's swap into the
  WETH storage token). Anyone may call `split()`; the reminder-worker
  calls it monthly. Funds can only ever leave into locks whose recipient
  is the owner. With B1 landed, the owner later claims each lock to any
  wallet. Build after a month of manual Save-page use shows the habit is
  real.
- **D2. ShieldVault** (replaces the separate quantum vault and the Stake &
  Shield v2 vault; `quantum-delay-vault` and `stake-shield-v2` in
  DEFERRED both resolve here). Standalone, opt-in, one per owner, holding
  whitelisted ERC-20s (wstETH first). Three behaviours, each enabled at
  creation: **soft exit** (`requestWithdraw` announces and starts a delay
  of N days; `withdraw` pays the owner after it; `cancelWithdraw` any
  time before); **inactivity fallback** (`checkin()` by the owner; after
  M months without one, named beneficiaries with shares can `claim`, the
  legacy semantics for an escrowed position, with the sponsored relay
  paying gas as for legacies); **quantum veto** (`veto(secret,
  recoveryTo)` during a pending withdrawal moves the whole balance to
  `recoveryTo` when `keccak256(secret)` equals the commitment registered
  for the owner in `QuantumRecoveryRegistry` before the request). Why one
  contract: Stake & Shield "via a soft timelock" was never possible
  because an escrowed position is out of the legacy's reach; the fallback
  puts inheritance inside the escrow. Phase 3 is the same mechanics as a
  Safe module. This is the contract behind the "bunker" door.
- **D3. ReferralRegistry**: append-only attribution proofs, QRR pattern,
  when a partner needs trustless proof.
- **D4. Staking-derived legacy** (`revenue-architecture.md`, EIP-7002
  Legacy Withdrawal Vault): validator withdrawal credentials pointed at a
  vault that forwards rewards and exits to the owner while alive and to
  the legacy's beneficiaries on activation. This is the concrete form of
  "an Ethereum legacy derived from staking rewards". Spec after D2.

## 10. ShieldVault UX (app side, planned with the contract)

One card in the Home grammar, honest sentences, no new vocabulary:

- **Entry**: from the quantum readiness card and the shield checks,
  "Protect a holding". The create flow asks: what to deposit (wstETH from
  Stake & Shield, or a whitelisted token); exit delay in days (presets);
  inactivity fallback (beneficiaries and shares with the legacy
  beneficiary UI, months of silence); quantum veto on or off. Veto on
  requires a registered recovery commitment; the flow offers to register
  one and prints the recovery sheet before the wallet ever opens.
- **The vault card**: balance, "exit delay N days", "fallback to your
  people after M months of silence", "quantum veto armed", one primary
  action: Withdraw, which states the wait before the signature.
- **A pending withdrawal**: a countdown on the card and an immediate
  reminder-worker email ("A withdrawal was requested from your
  ShieldVault. If this was not you, veto it now."). The wait is only
  useful if the owner notices; the alert is part of the product, not an
  extra.
- **Veto**: enter the secret from the recovery sheet (scan or type),
  choose the fresh wallet, one confirmation. The card then reads "Moved
  to safety on <date>" and offers to register a new commitment.
- **Fallback claim**: the legacy claim page pattern, gasless, with the
  same claim card for beneficiaries without a wallet.
- **Shield score**: a new check, "a protected holding", weighted like
  wallet coverage. The bunker door lands on this flow in focused mode.
